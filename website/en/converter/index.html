How to convert a date of birth to the Hebrew calendar

The Hebrew day begins not at midnight but at sunset. So for someone born in the evening, the Hebrew date is already the next one. If the time of birth is unknown, choose “before sunset” — that is the usual practice.

Why a Hebrew birthday “moves”

The Hebrew year is lunisolar: months follow the moon, and every few years a thirteenth month, Adar II, is added. That is why the same Hebrew date falls on a different Gregorian date every year.

How to calculate a yahrzeit

A yahrzeit is the anniversary of a loved one’s passing by the Hebrew calendar. The memorial candle is lit the evening before. If someone passed away in Adar of a regular year, Ashkenazi custom observes the yahrzeit in Adar I in a leap year. For the 30th of Cheshvan and Kislev the first-anniversary rule applies. The converter handles all of this.

How to calculate a bar mitzvah and bat mitzvah date

A bar mitzvah is at 13 and a bat mitzvah at 12, on the Hebrew birthday. For those born in Adar of a leap year, the date follows the last Adar.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a date of birth to the Hebrew calendar?

Enter the date and mark whether it was before or after sunset: a birth after sunset belongs to the next Hebrew date.

When is the bar mitzvah and bat mitzvah?

Bar mitzvah at 13, bat mitzvah at 12 by the Hebrew calendar, on the Hebrew birthday.

When is the yahrzeit candle lit?

The evening before: the Hebrew date begins at sunset.
